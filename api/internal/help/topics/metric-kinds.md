Each metric has a formula, a display format, a direction (higher or lower is better), a reporting frequency (monthly, quarterly, twice a year or annually), and the levels or item kinds it is reported at. Together these give its kind, which decides how it is charted:

- **Ratio**: a percentage judged by bands, such as the share of commitments met. Computed from summed parts, never an average of ratios.
- **Plan vs actual**: money against plan, as a percentage, with bands on both sides of 100% (under-spend and over-spend both matter).
- **Change**: judged on improvement against a reference (prior period, prior year or a baseline), shown as the improvement percentage.
- **Uptime**: a precise percentage reported for services or other tracked items.
- **Days**: a duration in days, such as an average age.
- **Discrete**: anything reported quarterly, twice a year or annually; one value per period.
- **Line**: everything else, such as counts.

A metric can also carry targets for a span of periods, a definition, notes, an example and a source. These come from the methodology version in force; read the metric's definition before explaining how it is measured.
